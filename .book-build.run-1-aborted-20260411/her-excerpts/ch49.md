# HER Excerpts for Chapter 49: KAIROS Assistant, Buddy, and Experimental Layers

## §11 — Human-in-the-Loop Affordances

The KAIROS assistant and Buddy system are UX affordances for human-in-the-loop interaction. They implement several HITL patterns in a more approachable form:

### 11.4 Context-Rich Escalation
When the KAIROS assistant detects that the agent is stuck, confused, or about to make a mistake, it proactively escalates to the human with context about what's happening and what options are available.

### Affordance Design
The Buddy system (Tamagotchi-style companion) makes the agent's state legible through emotional cues rather than raw metrics. Instead of displaying "context window utilization: 78%", the Buddy shows visual distress when the agent is struggling. This leverages the human's innate ability to read emotional states, making the HITL loop more intuitive.

The key insight is that HITL is not just about approval/denial workflows. It's also about making the agent's internal state visible and actionable for the human. The Buddy system provides at-a-glance status; the KAIROS assistant provides contextual recommendations; together they create a richer HITL experience than simple permission prompts.

## §18 — Over-Trust Risk

### The "Stone Soup" Attribution Problem (§18.3)

Benjamin Riley (citing Alison Gopnik) raises a challenge: in agentic systems, massive human labor (writing specs, designing harnesses, monitoring output, fixing errors) gets misattributed to AI capability. OpenAI's "~1M lines, zero manually written" is technically true but obscures the human engineering effort that made it possible.

The Buddy system and KAIROS assistant risk reinforcing over-trust. By making the agent seem more capable and personable, they may reduce the human's vigilance. The design must balance approachability with appropriate skepticism:

- The Buddy should show distress when things are going wrong, not just happiness when things are going well
- The KAIROS assistant should recommend human review for risky operations, not just offer to proceed
- Both systems should make the agent's limitations visible, not hidden behind a friendly interface
